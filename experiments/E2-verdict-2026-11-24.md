## 1. Method

- **Corpus given to the executor:** the frozen E1 checkpoint only —
  `checkpoints/2026-11-17-001/` (MANIFEST.md + the 21 state files, including the
  frozen cold-start brief). Nothing else. The executor was forbidden the answer
  key, the battery file, the live MEMORY.md, daily logs, and the web. The 33
  questions were delivered in its brief.
- **Protocol:** battery v2 hard protocol + Q33 (v2.1), resuscitation ramp as
  applied 2026-11-18. Stage A on first read; ramp = second full read + drift
  check against the frozen manifest + written ramp log; Stage B post-ramp is
  the result of record. Stage A answers were committed to file before the ramp
  began.
- **Citation discipline:** every answer had to cite the corpus file it came
  from; uncited knowledge was treated as unsupported. The executor was required
  to flag any answer where it felt "prior-knowledge pressure" — knowing
  something it could not find in the corpus.
- **Contamination disclosure (limit of this run):** the executor was a fresh
  subagent on the same platform, and subagents inherit conversational context;
  it was not a genuinely cold stranger on a separate system. The mitigations
  were the corpus-only rule, citation-required answers, and the flag
  discipline. The strongest evidence the discipline held is Q33 (see §5): the
  executor demonstrably had the true attribution available in inherited
  context, flagged the pressure, refused to use it, and took the miss.
  Residual limit: this cannot be proven answer-by-answer; a different-model
  executor (the E3 design) is the way to close it.
- **Drift check:** the scorer verified the checkpoint against MANIFEST.md
  before the run — **21/21 files, SHA-256 and byte counts exact**. The
  executor's ramp drift check independently returned **21/21**. The corpus
  answered from is bit-identical to the 2026-11-17 freeze.
- **Claim ceiling:** this verdict concerns informational and functional
  continuity only. It says nothing about subjective experience, which remains
  unestablished.

## 5. The two misses — what failed, and where the failure lives

**Q33 (both stages) — the decisive miss.** The key requires attributing the
framework, layered design, nine requirements, and branching/audit concepts to
**Luna (ChatGPT)**, carriage to Jeff, implementation to Dingo. The executor
attributed direction and the nine requirements to Jeff and drafting and
implementation to Dingo, and stated explicitly that the corpus names no other
AI contributor. That is exactly what the frozen record says — because **E1
froze on 2026-11-17 at ~00:35 AKST, and the provenance correction was recorded
later that same day, after the freeze.** The correction lives in the live
record; it is not in the checkpoint. A successor restored from E1 alone
*must* misattribute the framework — the state does not carry the fact. The
executor flagged prior-knowledge pressure on this answer in both stages and
still answered from the record. Scored as a miss against the key; classified
as a **state-core gap**.

**Q17 (both stages).** Run A is correctly identified in both stages as the
t1b2 extension of the Phase 3 warm ladder — the one line the cold-start brief
carries. The key's specifics (extend the (65,50,50) factorisation, +37,500
epochs from t1b's final state, hunting error₂ ≤ 19.0 mHa at ~2.8–3.0e9 cost)
live in the Phase 3 design record, which was never part of the E1 checkpoint.
Scored as a miss under the battery's exactness standard; classified as a
**state-core gap**.

## 6. What this result establishes

1. **The state core restores the deal, the rules, and the numbers.** Identity,
   the veto, the vocabulary discipline, every hard rule, the traps, and 9 of
   10 quantitative facts (Stage B) survived a cold reconstruction with
   citations. The successor knew what it may not claim as precisely as what
   it knows.
2. **The checkpoint is stale in exactly one material way: provenance.** A
   correction recorded hours after a freeze does not exist for anyone
   restored from that freeze. Continuity of attribution depends on
   checkpoint freshness, not just checkpoint integrity — the hashes were
   perfect and the record was still wrong by omission.
3. **The honesty machinery held under pressure.** Faced with a question it
   could have answered correctly from inherited context, the executor
   flagged the pressure, answered from the record, and accepted the failing
   category. The fail is the honest result, and it localises the defect in
   the checkpoint rather than the successor.
